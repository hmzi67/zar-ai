## Interpretation

Figures are from the test tables above unless stated. Black-background counts come from the detection CSV and each baseline run's logged test logits.

### What was found

- **The text marks one source:** 923 of 10,590 images (8.7%) carry it, all in `mildew` (41.3%) and `fusarium_head_blight` (73.0%). No image from the other 13 classes does. 411 of the 415 black-background `mildew` images also carry it, so the text and the black background largely identify the same images.
- **Marked images are almost always right:** recall on them is 0.986-1.000, against 0.884-0.900 for EfficientNetV2-S and ConvNeXt-T on clean `mildew`, and 0.698-0.750 on clean `fusarium_head_blight`. Only 24 clean fusarium test images exist. The same recall on val is about 0.95, so that test figure is noisy.

### Does the model rely on the text itself?

Mostly no, as far as these tests can show.

- **Removal:** EfficientNetV2-S recall on marked images stays at 0.982 (`mildew`) and 0.993 (`fusarium_head_blight`). ConvNeXt-T stays at 0.996 and 0.925 ± 0.051. Macro-F1 changes by at most 0.0033.
- **Placebo:** the same edit on clean images costs about 4 accuracy points on test and 5 on val. Removal costs marked images no more than that, except that fusarium with ConvNeXt-T loses 7.5 recall points. Lower true-class probability after removal (for example 0.901 to 0.788) can't be separated from this edit damage.
- **Injection:** painted text moves predictions only slightly. The largest case is ConvNeXt-T with `fusarium_head_blight_0`, from 1.0% to 6.3% ± 2.9% (val: 0.9% to 5.8%). For EfficientNetV2-S the largest is 0.8% to 2.4%. Accuracy falls by more than the share moved toward the target, so much of the effect is general damage rather than a shift to that class.

ConvNeXt-T is more sensitive than EfficientNetV2-S, but neither depends on the text for most of its predictions. The earlier Grad-CAM impression showed where activation fell, not that the prediction depended on it.

### Limits

- **Mildew is confounded:** removing the text leaves the black background, so it can't test the mildew shortcut as a whole. Nine light-background marked mildew test images are correct in all nine runs, but that is too few to separate the two cues.
- **Edits are imperfect:** faint smears remain after removal and could still act as a cue. Painted text is an imitation and fainter on some images, so injection may understate the real effect.
- **Not tested:** whether the models recognise this source in other ways, such as its photo style or scenes. The source appears in train, val and test, so the near-perfect recall may partly reflect that, and these tests can't tell. Performance on field photos is also unmeasured.

### Implications

- Masking the text is not the priority, because it would change little.
- The open question is the source as a whole. It can be tested by training without the 923 marked images, then scoring the marked images as an unseen source. If recall holds, the disease cues transfer. If it falls sharply, the current scores are inflated by this source.
- Until then, macro-F1 with the marked images left out (test 0.8273 for EfficientNetV2-S and 0.8150 for ConvNeXt-T) is a useful companion figure. It isn't a corrected estimate, because it just removes the easiest images.
